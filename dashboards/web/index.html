Which used cars are priced out of line, which are worth buying, and what moves the price?

A pricing desk for a used-car dealer in Brazil, built on the InfoCars showcase. Each ad is matched to a catalogue of make, model, version and year, checked for bad prices, and compared with every other ad for the same car. The page shows what comparable cars are listed for, how the asking price changes the time on the site, which private-seller ads a dealer should buy, what moves the price (kilometres, seller, gearbox, photos), and which ads online are likely to get stuck.

Finding 1Cars priced under the market sell in about half the time.

Ads more than 6% below the median of comparable ads sold in a median of 17 days, and 74% of them within 30 days. Ads more than 6% above took 30 days, and only 51% sold within 30 days.

Finding 282 ads still online are priced above the market.

On 1 November 2025 they ask R$ 530,500 more than their comparable medians in total, a median of 7.8% each, and have been online for a median of 50.5 days.

Finding 331 ads still online are priced below the market.

They ask R$ 156,950 less than their comparable medians, a median of 6.1% each. They should sell fast, but the gap is margin a dealer may not need to give away.

Finding 434 private-seller ads online are at or below their market median; 4 are a buy at the asking price.

Bought at a target offer of 90% of the comparable median, the 23 ads in Buy now and Negotiate would clear R$ 137,197 after preparation, a median of R$ 5,548 per car. Private sellers ask 2.0% under the comparable median and dealers 1.8% over.

Finding 5Same car, same year: each 10,000 km takes about R$ 5,840 (6.5%) off the asking price.

A least-squares fit of price on km inside each version and model year, over 699 ads in groups of 8 or more. Kilometres explain 48% of the price spread; ads with 16 or more photos sit 1.8% above the market, colour and fuel move it by 2% at most.

Finding 6Half of the ads leave the site within 30 days; the 87 that cut their price sold a median of 9 days after the cut.

46% of ads are still online after 30 days and 12% after 90. 15 ads online score 60 or more on the stock-risk scale (price, days online, kilometres): R$ 68,200 of cuts would bring them back into range.

Finding 733 of 1,200 ads never reach the statistics.

15 have no price or one out of range, and 18 sit far from comparable cars, usually a price typed without its last digit. 187 clean ads were matched only through typo correction, and 116 make and model spellings reduce to 7 models.

Buy list: which private-seller ads are worth buying this morning?

Ads from private sellers still online, asked at or below the median of comparable ads. A dealer pays about 90% of the market price (the target offer) and spends about 2% on preparation; the margin is the comparable median less what is paid less preparation. Buy now: 8% or more under the median. Negotiate: 3% to 8%. Wait: under 3%.

How it is built

  • The data are the 1,200 synthetic ads of the showcase repository, generated with a fixed seed. Titles copy the mistakes of real ads; prices, mileage and dates are invented. The generator makes cars priced below market leave the site faster, so the first chart checks that the pipeline recovers that pattern. Nothing on this page is a result of the real business.
  • Titles are matched by the repository's own Python matcher (whole-word aliases, then fuzzy matching for typos). dbt models on DuckDB then apply the quality checks, the market statistics and the position against the market, and reproduce the demo's numbers exactly: 1,167 clean ads, 187 through typo correction, and a 2023 Onix LTZ market of 6 ads with a median of R$ 68,400.
  • An ad that disappears counts as sold on its last day online. Days to sell count sold ads only, so slow cars that are still online make the gap between price bands smaller, not larger.
  • In production InfoCars held about 250,000 listings, cut 5,114 model spellings to 641 real models, and the dealer measured a saving of about R$ 2,000 per negotiation, with time in stock down to one to three months.
  • Version 2 (November 2026) adds to the generator the attributes the production table had: seller type, gearbox, fuel, colour, photos, city and a price history. Every version 1 column is unchanged, so the numbers above did not move; the new columns are drawn from a second generator conditioned on each ad's distance to its market, which plants the patterns the buy list, the price drivers and the stock-risk score look for. The km depreciation, the buy list, the liquidity curve and the risk score are dbt marts; this page recomputes them from the same ads in the browser, and the notebook checks they agree.
  • The same star schema (ads, version, state, date) feeds a Power BI model in TMDL with DAX measures and a Qlik Sense app with set analysis.
